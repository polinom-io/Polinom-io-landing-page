Most consultancies sell one term at a time. You hire a strategy firm for the roadmap, a dev shop to build it, a data team to instrument it, and then discover that nobody owns the seams between them. The roadmap doesn't match what's technically feasible on your timeline; the build doesn't expose the metrics the strategy deck promised; the data pipeline ships three months after the feature it was supposed to measure.

We treat this as one problem, not three handoffs — a single equation with four terms:

```
P(business) = Strategy·x³ + Engineering·x² + Data·x + Execution
```

Each term is a real service line — strategy and advisory, software engineering, data and AI, execution and delivery — but the point of writing it as a polynomial rather than a menu is the *weighting*. Every business's equation has different coefficients. A pre-seed startup validating a wedge needs a large Execution term and almost no Strategy term: ship the thing, see what happens. A company migrating off a decade-old platform needs the Engineering term to dominate, with Data close behind to make sure nothing regresses silently. Neither needs all four terms at equal weight, and neither should pay for weight they don't need.

**How the weighting actually gets set.** It's not a guess and it's not a fixed-price package matched to your company size. The first part of any engagement — the Discover step in our process — is explicitly about defining the variables: your market constraints, your existing stack, what your team can already do well versus what's a genuine gap. The coefficients come out of that, not out of a rate card.

**Why this matters more than it sounds.** The actual failure mode we're designing against isn't "bad strategy" or "bad code" in isolation — it's the seam between terms. A roadmap with no engineering input promises timelines that don't exist. An engineering build with no data term ships blind. Execution with no strategy term optimizes the wrong thing quickly. Decomposing the problem into named terms doesn't just make the pitch legible — it's a forcing function to make sure someone is explicitly responsible for the connective tissue, not just each term separately.
